Which forecasting model should you trust?

Adoption is held back less by access than by confidence.

Google (TimesFM), Amazon (Chronos), Salesforce (Moirai) and others have released pretrained forecasting models. Many are open and run on ordinary hardware. What's missing is an independent answer to which one to trust.

Vendors grade their own work

Each model's authors choose the benchmarks they report.

Leaderboards are general

They score public datasets, not the series a central bank, utility or retailer actually forecasts.

Models turn over fast

A new release every few months leaves practitioners unable to keep up.

The gap: a neutral, domain-focused center that evaluates, adapts and explains these models, and keeps doing so as they change.

What foundation models change

A time series foundation model is one large model, pretrained once on billions of data points, that can forecast series it has never seen. It does for forecasting what large language models did for text.

Classical methods fit a separate model to each series. Foundation models learn general patterns (trend, seasonality, level shifts, noise) from a huge mix of real and synthetic data, then read a window of recent history and return a forecast with uncertainty bands.

Zero-shot means giving the model a series' recent history and asking for the next steps, with no training. Fine-tuning trains it further on a domain's series; it can help, but it isn't always worth the cost. Knowing when each approach wins is the evidence this center will produce.

Every forecast comes with confidence intervals

A forecast is only useful if you know how much to trust it. We go beyond the intervals a model produces on its own.

  1. Native quantile forecasts. Models such as Chronos-2 and TimesFM predict a range of outcomes directly.
  2. Conformal prediction. Intervals are adjusted from recent forecast errors so they reach their stated coverage: a 90% interval should contain the actual value about 90% of the time.
  3. Ensemble intervals. The spread across several models captures uncertainty about which model is right.
  4. Ongoing calibration checks. As new data arrives, we test whether intervals are too narrow or too wide and correct them.

Vision

Every organization that forecasts knows which model to trust for its data, and how much to trust each forecast.

Mission

To be the trusted, independent source of evidence on how time series foundation models perform in real domains, and to help organizations turn those forecasts into better decisions.

Neutral by design

Every model runs under the same rules, and results are published whether or not they favor any vendor.

Calibrated uncertainty

Ranges, not single numbers, with intervals that are checked against what actually happened.

Open methods

Evaluation code, curated datasets and methods are public, so anyone can check our work.

Four programs on one shared pipeline

Each program produces public output and builds on the same data and evaluation pipeline.

Flagship

Independent evaluation hub

Tests every major model side by side, under identical conditions, on domain data such as macroeconomic releases, energy load and retail demand.

Produces monthly out-of-sample scorecards of accuracy and interval reliability.

Domain adaptation research

Fine-tunes open-weight models on curated domain data and measures what helps, such as handling data revisions, short histories and regime breaks.

Produces papers, reproducible code and adapted weights where licenses allow.

Ensembles and decision science

Combines foundation models with classical baselines and links forecast accuracy to decision quality.

Produces methods, case studies and decision playbooks.

Education and access

A hosted sandbox to run every model on your own data, plus workshops, tutorials and a companion textbook.

Produces courses, tutorials and an open sandbox.

Questions and answers

1.Macroeconomic nowcasts already exist, such as the Atlanta Fed's GDPNow. Why do we need this?

Nowcasts and our work answer different questions, and they complement each other.

Nowcasts (e.g., GDPNow)This center
HorizonThe current quarter, before the official releaseMonths to years ahead
CoverageA few headline numbers, mainly GDP and inflationThousands of regional, sector and industry series
MethodHand-built models that mirror how agencies compile GDPGeneral pretrained models that need no per-series design
UncertaintyVaries by providerCalibrated confidence intervals on every forecast

Benchmark, not rival. Nowcasts become baselines in our scorecards. Where a nowcast performs well, we say so; where foundation models add value, as extra inputs to nowcasts or for longer horizons, we show it with evidence.

2.Why not just use the vendors' own results?

Each vendor picks the benchmarks it reports. We test every model under the same conditions, on domain data, and publish the results whether or not they favor any model.

3.Will foundation models replace classical methods?

Not always. On stable series with long histories, ARIMA and exponential smoothing remain strong. Our scorecards show where each approach wins, which is the evidence users need.

4.What happens when a better model comes out next year?

We add it. The evaluation pipeline is built to score a new model within weeks of release, so the center becomes more valuable as models change.

5.Is client data safe?

Client data used for fine-tuning or sandbox runs stays private. Only anonymized, aggregate results enter our research, and only with client consent.

Hardware: modest is enough to start

These models are small next to large language models. Chronos-2 has 120M parameters and produces over 300 forecasts per second on a single A10G GPU, and it also runs on CPU. TimesFM 2.5 needs about 800 MB of disk.
